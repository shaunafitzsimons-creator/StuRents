# Numbers and Sizing

> Cost, revenue, and operational load implications of ongoing sanctions monitoring.

---

## Current state (baseline)

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Tenants signing contracts via ABC annually | 200,000 |
| Tenants with rent facilitated via ABC | 100,000 |
| Revenue per sanctions check (charged to letting agent) | £2.00 |
| Cost per sanctions check | £0.10 |
| Total annual check revenue | £400,000 |
| Total annual check cost | £20,000 |
| Gross margin on checks | £380,000 (95%) |

The current model is highly profitable on a per-check basis. The new requirement threatens this margin unless ongoing monitoring is priced into the product.

---

## Cost of ongoing monitoring

Ongoing monitoring applies to the **100,000 tenants** with active rent payments. The legislation does not specify check frequency — the table below models three scenarios.

| Frequency | Checks/year | Cost/year | vs. current cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly (minimum defensible) | 1,200,000 | £120,000 | +£100,000 |
| Weekly | 5,200,000 | £520,000 | +£500,000 |
| Daily | 36,500,000 | £3,650,000 | +£3,630,000 |

**Monthly is the right starting frequency.** It aligns to the payment cycle (the point of maximum exposure), is defensible to regulators as "ongoing," and keeps costs manageable. Weekly and daily would require a fundamental repricing of the product to be commercially viable.

At monthly frequency: the new ongoing monitoring adds **£120,000/year** in direct check costs — a 6x increase on the current cost base. This cannot be absorbed without a pricing change.

## Revenue model

Currently, letting agents pay £2.00 per tenant once, at contract signing. There is no recurring charge.

If ABC charges for ongoing monitoring as a separate line item:

| Price per tenant per month | Annual revenue (100,000 tenants) | Annual cost (monthly checks) | Net margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| £0.15 | £180,000 | £120,000 | £60,000 |
| £0.25 | £300,000 | £120,000 | £180,000 |
| £0.50 | £600,000 | £120,000 | £480,000 |
| £1.00 | £1,200,000 | £120,000 | £1,080,000 |

Alternatively, ABC could bundle ongoing monitoring into a revised per-tenant fee at contract signing — accepting the cost as a service improvement. At £120,000/year across 200,000 contract signings, the break-even addition to the upfront fee is **£0.60 per tenant**. Raising the contract signing fee from £2.00 to £2.60 would cover the cost with no margin improvement.

The stronger commercial argument is a recurring monthly charge — it converts a one-time transaction into a subscription revenue stream and more accurately reflects the ongoing service being delivered.

## Operational load estimate

The current human review volume is unknown (not provided in the brief — this is a key question for operations). However, we can model the impact.

Assumption: X% of automated checks score ≥ 0.70 and require human review.

| Flag rate | Current reviews/year (200k checks) | Additional reviews/year (1.2m monthly checks) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5% | 1,000 | 6,000 | 7,000 |
| 1% | 2,000 | 12,000 | 14,000 |
| 2% | 4,000 | 24,000 | 28,000 |

At a 1% flag rate, ongoing monitoring generates **1,000 additional manual reviews per month** on top of the current baseline. At 5–60 minutes per review, this represents between **83 and 1,000 hours of manual review per month** — a range that spans "manageable with the current team" to "requires dedicated headcount."

**This is the single biggest operational risk in the new requirement.** Before committing to a monthly check cadence, the actual flag rate from current data must be established. If it's above 1%, the manual review model needs to change — either through automation, triage prioritisation, or additional resource.

---

## Key numbers to remember

- Monthly monitoring costs **£120,000/year** at minimum defensible frequency
- Break-even upfront fee increase: **+£0.60 per tenant**
- At 1% flag rate, ongoing monitoring adds up to **1,000 manual reviews/month**
- The 8-hour payment window (9am–5pm) is the constraint that makes **speed of review** commercially and legally critical
